I woke up to the news that the great [Perry Mehrling](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Perry_Mehrling) has [passed away](https://www.ineteconomics.org/perspectives/blog/remembering-perry-mehrling). He was 67. I was shocked. It feels unfair that all the cool and awesome people keep getting taken away while assholes still roam free on Earth.

Perry was brilliant. A lot of what I understand about global macro, the dollar and the global financial system comes from listening to him. The very first time I heard him, I fell in love with his heterodox view of the global financial system. It was refreshing. I was sick and tired of the same old takes from people in macro, mostly academics.

Perry's view was different, and you could see the insane amount of work that had gone into it. His "money view" was sophisticated and built on the work of [Hyman Minsky](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hyman_Minsky), [Charles Kindleberger](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Charles_P._Kindleberger) and others. His last book, *Money and Empire*, was about Kindleberger and the dollar system.

Predictably, everyone is sharing his famous INET videos, and rightly so. If Perry is in a video, it's worth watching. But for me, most of what I know about the dollar-based financial system and the centrality of the dollar comes from his many interviews and podcasts over the years.

What struck me most was how intellectually honest and open he was. He was generous in sharing what he knew and, more importantly, willing to admit what he didn't. That was the first thing I felt grateful for when I heard the news. Plenty of academics love sharing their research, but you'd be surprised how many have an "oh my precious baby" syndrome about their work. Perry just wanted to teach people.

At the very least, I owe him a deep debt of gratitude. A large part of how I understand money has been shaped by listening to him.

Perry, I wish you were still around, doing all those podcasts and interviews so I could keep learning from you. Rest in peace, and thank you for so graciously sharing everything you knew.
